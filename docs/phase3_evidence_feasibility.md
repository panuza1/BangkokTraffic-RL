# Phase 3 — historical evidence and calibration feasibility

**Decision for review, 6 November 2026:** the current scenario is a *Bangkok-informed synthetic benchmark*, not a calibrated or validated reconstruction of 2 June 2016. The sources support a constrained historical investigation and a future calibration attempt, but do not yet support identifying its routes, boundary demand, initial queues, lane-level connections and signal clearances for that day. Keep the current baseline unchanged and stop before Phase 4. No ablation outcome was used to select baseline parameters. The held-out test day 9 June 2016 was not used to change the model; the published Fig. 24 contains daily rows for the month including that date, but no result from that row was extracted or used here.

## What Figures 4, 24 and 25 establish

- **Fig. 4 / phase descriptions:** eight nominal phase movement diagrams at Sathorn–Surasak. The text describes phase 1 as both Sathorn directions; phase 3 as Surasak straight/right, with a simultaneous left turn described in the text; and phase 5 as specified right-turn flows from Surasak and Charoen Rat. Other phases are described as partial movements associated with spillback. These are movement descriptions, not SUMO link-index mappings, 2016 clearance timing, or permission to infer every lane connection. Diagram arrows and textual description should be reconciled with authority-provided signal plans before implementation.
- **Fig. 24:** a heatmap of phase occurrence counts **by date** for June 2016. It is not one monthly total, and the colors do not provide exact phase transition times or phase durations. Do not use another day's row as 2 June evidence, and do not use the held-out 9 June row to tune the model.
- **Fig. 25:** hourly sequence counts on **2 June 2016**; the article describes frequent `1–3–5` and `1–3–1–5` patterns. It is evidence of patterns over an hour, not the exact sequence or clearance intervals during every 15-minute window. It cannot prove the state at 07:00 or map real phases to model links.

The separate **2 June 2016** signal CSV contains, for example, `06:59:09,2,5,60`, `07:00:08,2,6,15`, and `07:00:20,2,1,65` (fields Time, Mode, Phase, Duration). Scientific Data Table 12 explicitly states **1 = automatic, 2 = manual** and describes duration as phase operation time. This resolves the earlier audit's uncertainty about mode coding, not the exact transition/clearance semantics: successive timestamp differences do not always equal reported duration (e.g. 12 s between the latter two timestamps versus 15 s reported). Boundary state, data latency, loss, and whether yellow/all-red are included still require documentation. The separate Table 4's explicit start-time example applies to **2014 manual-collection files** and must not automatically be transferred to the 2016 controller log. Times lack a timezone offset; Bangkok local time remains an assumption.

## Evidence classification against the current SUMO case

**Observed** means reported in a specified source or inspected dated record, not independently field-verified here. **Inferred** is a conclusion derived from observations. **Assumed** is a model choice without validation. **Unknown** means unresolved for the 2 June 2016 07:00–07:15 case. These labels can coexist in one row.

| Component | Observed / inferred evidence | Current SUMO assumption and remaining unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Signal phases | **Observed:** Fig. 4 eight phase descriptions; the 2 June controller log records numbered phase events and mode 2. Fig. 25 shows same-day hourly patterns. | **Assumed:** five generated green groups, 12 s each. **Unknown:** mapping between each historical phase and all 30 SUMO controlled link indices, lane permissions, and exact state at the start/end of the chosen window. Five groups do not establish reproduction of the eight historical phases. |
| Timing and transitions | **Observed:** 2 June event times and reported durations; Table 12 identifies mode and duration. **Inferred:** variable manually operated timings are plausible, but event differences need interpretation. | **Assumed:** fixed 90 s cycle, 4 s yellow, 2 s all-red. **Unknown:** actual yellow, all-red, transition rules, phase timestamp/duration alignment and pedestrian treatment on the target day. No clearance may be reduced to force a fit. |
| Lane connections / turns | **Observed:** 2018 authors state joined-junction auto-connections sometimes required correction; 2017 paper supplies movement-level 2014 examples. **Inferred:** automatic current-map connectivity needs review against historic plans. | **Assumed:** imported current OSM and SUMO-generated connections. Link 3's modeled final turn connects from lane 0; Link 6's from lanes 0–1. **Unknown:** whether these connections were legal and physically correct in 2016, or whether simulated route/edge identity corresponds to the historical movements. |
| Sensor mapping | **Observed:** 2022 Fig. 3 places 17 loop/thermal points across approaches, approximately 30 m from junction; source notebook groups IDs by approach. IEEE paper characterizes specific sensor groups by turn/through movement for its study. | **Assumed:** approximate SUMO loops on every lane of selected edges. **Unknown:** exact coordinates, sensor-to-SUMO lane and movement mapping, overlap or completeness of coverage, and equivalence of real and modeled lane topology. Movement labels from another study are evidence for investigation, not an automatic one-to-one mapping. |
| Turning ratios / demand | **Observed:** 2014 manual dataset describes L/S/R/U counts; the 2017 paper gives movement counts for its cited historic source and different afternoon windows. **Inferred:** 100% of an approach on one synthetic route may misrepresent actual movement mix. | **Assumed:** one route per approach, constant arrivals equal to 0.8 × 2 June observed counts converted to an hourly rate. **Unknown:** 2 June 07:00–07:15 boundary arrivals and actual turn proportions; detector passage counts are not directly boundary inflow. No cross-year or other-day proportions should be relabeled as 2 June observations. |
| Initial queues / spillback | **Observed:** 2014 manual queue records are described; 2022 Fig. 20 shows an estimated/interpolated queue on **1 October 2016**, not 2 June. The 2018 authors describe matching initial queues to the collection day of their own model and a 15-minute evening injection. | **Assumed:** empty network at time 0 and no warm-up/drain. **Unknown:** independently measured queues and downstream states at 07:00 on 2 June, including vehicles beyond this small boundary. Different-date estimates and another model's backlogs are not same-day observations. |
| Vehicle mix, detector quality and external targets | **Observed:** 2 June loop files are five-second counts/occupancy, with the selected interval complete; article mentions taxi GPS for six days including 2 June, and thermal sensors have the same nominal location. | **Assumed:** passenger-only cars, constant flow, seed 42. **Unknown:** matched 2 June trip/queue observations, vehicle mix, usable GPS coverage and independence, sensor-to-arrival relationship, and unbiased detector coverage. GPS suitability has not been assessed and must not be claimed. |

The 2017 paper's reported historical movement counts, 2018 Chula-SSS travel-time fit, and 2022 signal plots are distinct evidence, not a single synchronized field observation. Likewise, a paper's claimed calibration or a simulation-generated queue is not validation of the present network.

## Gap analysis and decision gates

| Priority | Evidence required | Why it matters / acceptable next step |
|---|---|---|
| Blocking | Historical junction geometry, legal lane-to-lane turns, stop lines and authoritative phase-to-movement mapping, with 2016 validity | Match Fig. 4 and physical lanes to SUMO controlled links; verify conflicts, pedestrian and transition rules against authoritative sources. Do not infer added turns from queue reduction. |
| Blocking | Documented source event timestamp semantics, duration, controller mode and yellow/all-red clearances; 2 June boundary phase | Reconstruct and audit the time-aligned signal program. Keep existing safety assumptions until independently substantiated; modeled conflict checks alone are not field safety validation. |
| Blocking | Boundary inflows, time-resolved turns, vehicle mix and downstream conditions on 2 June or defensible bounded estimation | Prevent non-identifiability: detector discharge, arrivals, route choice and external queues cannot all be recovered uniquely from four aggregated counts. Retain counts as observations, not boundary truth. |
| Blocking for measured initialization | Same-day, same-time upstream/downstream queue or occupancy observations and network boundary coverage | Establish a realistic starting state and compare a documented warm-up or queue injection; do not substitute the 2014 or 1 October 2016 data as the target day's queue. |
| Needed for validation | Independently mapped targets such as 2 June queue/travel time and a distinct validation day, plus measurement quality | Audit the six-day GPS/thermal files without inspecting held-out 9 June for tuning; check spatial/time coverage, denominators and independent evidence. Same-source or fitted measurements alone are not independent validation. |
| Protocol | Fixed objective metrics and acceptance thresholds, uncertainty bounds, warm-up/drain, seed replications, split and artifact hashes | Freeze before parameter fitting; train on the chosen training data, evaluate independently on validation, reserve 9 June for final locked testing. Explain gaps and uncertainty rather than optimize against the test set. |
| Reuse | Explicit permission or clear license for Chula-SSS code/scenario; confirm SUMO version compatibility if reuse is authorized | The 2018 paper describes SUMO 0.25.0 while this project uses 1.28.0; neither file availability nor author calibration guarantees legal or technical portability. |

**Recommendation:** use the existing case only as an explicitly synthetic, Bangkok-informed benchmark for sensitivity and software checks while acquiring the missing evidence. If the blockers are resolved, prepare a documented calibration protocol, then fit using training data and independently validate before making any calibrated claim. If they cannot be resolved, retain a synthetic benchmark with bounded assumptions and avoid claims about historical controller performance or real-world safety. Neither path authorizes Phase 4 now.
